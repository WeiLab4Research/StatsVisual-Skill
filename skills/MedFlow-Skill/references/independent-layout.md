# Attractive layout without a reference image

Use the approved research content as the starting point. No matching paper figure is required. The eight priority folders are references, not a limit on design ability; the custom route remains available. Visual style controls appearance only.

## Compose before placing coordinates

1. **Choose a reading spine.** Prefer downward progression for successive stages, left-to-right progression for a short sequence, parallel lanes for comparable streams, and a hierarchy or grouped network for nonsequential relationships. Choose from the content, not from a fixed template.
2. **Separate levels.** Put major stages on the main path, exclusions beside the relevant transition, and supporting details within their associated boxes. Preserve all substantive paths, conditions and merges. Dense content may need a larger canvas or panels, not omitted study information.
3. **Establish alignment.** Align main box centres or edges, repeat comparable widths, and align equivalent stages across lanes. Let long side boxes have their own height. For a quiet regular appearance, use a small number of width groups rather than a unique arbitrary width for every node.
4. **Allocate breathing room.** Keep text comfortably inside boxes. Leave visible space before and after side branches, around arrowheads and between columns. Long reason lists require a taller transition band. Shorten long empty stems that carry no structural purpose; do not compress all gaps to the minimum.
5. **Route cleanly.** Avoid crossings and routes through unrelated boxes. Attach exclusions to the main line, and keep the main continuation visually obvious. Preserve the exact study relation even if its route requires more space. A clean orthogonal route is usually preferable to unnecessary bends.
6. **Use a restrained visual hierarchy.** Pick one coherent font family, a few text levels, consistent strokes and a small role-based palette from the selected style. Emphasis should support the approved message. Do not add coloured categories, counts, icons or legends merely for decoration.
7. **Balance the whole composition.** Compare left/right mass, top/bottom density and competing columns. Centre a shared ancestor over its children; make genuine splits and merges visible. Accept purposeful asymmetry when branch content differs instead of forcing an empty symmetric grid.
8. **Review once as a reader.** Trace the figure without the source text: is the start apparent, are paths and attachments clear, are all labels readable, and does the page feel neither cramped nor sparse? Fix identified problems, not tiny numeric differences.

## Proportions, not exact measurements

At an ordinary manuscript scale, roughly 9–11 pt text, about half to one text-height of inner padding, and one to three text-heights between simple peer elements can be useful starting proportions. A side box often needs more transition space. These are suggestions, not limits or journal mandates. Adjust by the amount of text, actual final size and the selected visual style.

Outer padding should look small and even without clipping. Internal whitespace is part of the composition and may be generous where it clarifies the reading path. Do not minimise the bounding rectangle at the expense of breathing room.

## Rendering approach

For simple layouts, use automatic ranks/lanes and existing defaults, then make local adjustments. For a complex figure, choose explicit regions and coordinates or project-specific vector code. Exact coordinate optimisation, exhaustive layout searches and repeated font/spacing sweeps are not required. The renderer is a construction tool, not proof that the composition is attractive.

Preserve user confirmation, study completeness, trunk attachment correctness, no clipped/overlapping text, no connectors through unrelated boxes, and editable SVG/PPTX plus vector PDF delivery. No canvas title or explanatory annotation is added. Do not claim pixel-identical output or journal certification.
